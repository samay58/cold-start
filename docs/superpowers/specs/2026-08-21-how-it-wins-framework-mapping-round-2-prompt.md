### Canonical framework: 80 strategies in 13 groups

Use these exact names and meanings. Do not rename, merge, or invent strategies.

#### Accumulation

- Usership: A critical mass of users makes the product more useful to each of them.
- Completeness: One tool covers everything the buyer needs, so nothing else is required.
- Aggregation: An environment or marketplace built for broad participation.
- Diversification: Low dependency on any one stream of customers, suppliers, or money.
- Omnipresence: Available everywhere it might be needed and accepted by default.
- Cloning: Expands by replicating identical copies of itself.

#### Price

- Affordability: Costs less than the alternatives.
- Luxury: Costs deliberately more, often labor-intensive or a status marker.
- Skimming: Takes a large volume of tiny transactions or resources.
- Bundling: Comes packaged free with something people already want.

#### Time

- Heritage: A legacy that spans generations.
- Craftsmanship: Spends more time, precision, and attention than competitors do.
- Organic: Grows through a natural process that cannot be accelerated.
- Endurance: Keeps operating continuously over a long period.

#### Uniqueness

- Specialization: Strong competence in a narrow niche.
- Versatility: General competence adapted to many tasks.
- Hybrid: Competence in two distinct areas, or two strengths not usually found together.
- Divergence: Creativity and difference from the norm.
- Authenticity: Traceable to its source where counterfeits are common.
- Rarity: Naturally limited in quantity.
- Scarcity: Artificially limited in quantity.
- Secrecy: Hard to copy because the knowledge is protected or the parts cannot be disentangled.
- Irreverence: Contrarian and counter-cultural, disregarding tradition.

#### Offense

- Violence: Destroys or consumes the opponent through force.
- Litigation: Weakens opponents through legal burden.
- Nettlesomeness: Causes the opponent to make mistakes.
- Sabotage: Creates weaknesses in the opponent's structure or defenses.
- Parasitism: Extracts or hijacks resources from a host.
- Scavenging: Opportunistically takes in dying or weakened prey.
- Espionage: Acquires the opponent's secrets.
- Swarming: A concentrated attack from many individually weak agents.
- Highest bidder: Overpays or outbids competitors to secure exclusivity.
- Chokepoint: Controls a passage that competitors or prey must pass through.
- Puppeteering: Takes over a host's behavior and directs it.

#### Defense

- Deterrence: Projects a credible threat of retaliation.
- Reliability: Reduces maintenance and downtime.
- Predictability: A repeatable process with little deviation.
- Unpredictability: Creates surprise, confusion, or variability.
- Decentralization: Redundancy and distributed competence remove single points of failure.
- Security: Resists theft, confiscation, and unwanted access.
- Privacy: Protects against unwanted disclosure.
- Durability: Physically strong and resistant to damage.
- Neutrality: Displays long-term non-belligerence and offers a safe haven.
- Obscurity: Survives by remaining unknown or undetected.
- Antifragility: Gets stronger when exposed to stress or usage.

#### Deception

- Camouflage: Blends into the surrounding environment.
- Mimicry: Superficially adopts another's characteristics to mislead.
- Decoy: Distracts or misleads adversaries.
- Lure: Sets attractive traps.
- Infiltration: Gets past defenses without notice.

#### Timing

- First-mover: Acts first to gain an advantage.
- Second-mover: Moves quickly to copy the first mover.
- Last-mover: Waits until opponents have spent themselves on failed approaches.

#### Accreditation

- Monopoly: Control of a resource or market approved by a governing body.
- Prestige: Endorsed by authoritative sources through awards, degrees, or recognition.
- Curation: Selective, with a particular ability to choose and group.

#### Collaboration

- Union: Reduces friction between potential competitors through a common set of rules.
- Alliance: A partnership with some benefits of a union while staying independent.
- Emergence: The wisdom of a crowd.
- Centralization: A single decision-making entity.
- Standardization: Emergent alignment that reduces friction.
- Symbiosis: A mutually beneficial dependency between two organisms.
- Herding: Many individuals grouping to protect against larger competitors.
- Distributed ownership: Owned by the community.
- Transparency: An open and visible process that invites trust.

#### Speed and scale

- Iteration: Iterates and changes quickly.
- Efficiency: Uses fewer resources than competitors for similar capability.
- Agility: Adapts easily to a changing environment.
- Precision: High accuracy and exactness in performance or output.
- Blitzing: A sudden, concentrated expenditure of intense resources.
- Composability: Components and systems assemble in different configurations.
- Modularity: Independent units that combine in different ways.

#### Ease

- Intuitiveness: Easy to use and understand without instruction.
- Fun: Provides enjoyment and amusement.
- Simplicity: Few points of failure and a minimal design.
- Low-friction: Minimal resistance or hassle in use.
- Charm: Creates loyalty and warmth through personality or aesthetics.

#### Transformation

- Malleability: Changes appearance or form; easily modified or customized.
- Metamorphosis: Transforms between states optimized for different functions.
- Copycat: Replicates another's method or style.
