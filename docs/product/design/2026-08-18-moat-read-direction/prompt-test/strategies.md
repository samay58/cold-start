Accumulation: Usership (a critical mass of users, network effect); Completeness (all-in-one tool, one-stop shop); Aggregation (an environment or marketplace designed for broad participation); Diversification (low dependency on any one stream of resources); Omnipresence (available everywhere it might be needed, accepted by default); Cloning (expands by replicating identical copies of itself).
Price: Affordability (comparatively low cost); Luxury (intentionally high cost, often labor-intensive, or status symbol); Skimming (ingests a large volume of very small transactions or resources); Bundling (packaged freely with something desirable).
Time: Heritage (multi-generational legacy); Craftsmanship (uses more time, precision, and attention to detail than competitors); Organic (natural growth process that cannot be accelerated); Endurance (ability to operate continuously over a long period of time).
Uniqueness: Specialization (strong competence in a narrow niche); Versatility (general competence adapted to many tasks); Hybrid (competence in two distinct areas, or blending of two strengths not usually found together); Divergence (creativity, unusualness, differentiation from the norm); Authenticity (traceable to its source, especially where counterfeiting is prevalent); Rarity (naturally limited in quantity); Scarcity (artificially limited in quantity); Secrecy (made difficult to copy by protecting private knowledge, or combining components in a way that cannot be disentangled); Irreverence (contrarian, counter-cultural, disregard for tradition).
Offense: Violence (destroys or consumes the opponent through force); Litigation (weakens opponents through legal burden); Nettlesomeness (causes the opponent to make mistakes); Sabotage (creates weaknesses in the opponent's structure or defenses); Parasitism (extracts or hijacks resources from a host); Scavenging (opportunistically ingests dying or weakened prey); Espionage (acquires enemy secrets); Swarming (concentrated attack from a mass of individually weak agents); Highest bidder (overpays or outbids competitors to secure exclusivity); Chokepoint (controls a passage that competitors or prey must pass through); Puppeteering (takes over a host's behavior and directs it to serve you).
Defense: Deterrence (mutually assured destruction, projecting fear of violence or litigation); Reliability (reduces maintenance costs, minimizes downtime); Predictability (repeatable process with little deviation to create a consistent product or experience); Unpredictability (creating an element of surprise, confusion, or variability); Decentralization (redundancy and distributed competency to reduce single points of failure); Security (resistance to theft, confiscation, unwanted access); Privacy (protection against unwanted information disclosure); Durability (physical robustness, resistance to damage); Neutrality (displays long-term non-belligerence, a safe haven); Obscurity (survival by remaining unknown or undetected); Antifragility (becomes stronger when exposed to stress or usage).
Deception: Camouflage (ability to blend into the surrounding environment); Mimicry (superficially adopts characteristics of another to trick opponents); Decoy (distracts or misleads adversaries); Lure (preys on others by setting attractive traps); Infiltration (gets past defenses without notice).
Timing: First-mover (acts first to gain an advantage); Second-mover (fast follower, moves quickly to copy the first mover); Last-mover (waits until all opponents have expended energy on failed approaches).
Accreditation: Monopoly (artificial control of a resource or market approved by the governing body); Prestige (endorsed by authoritative sources via awards, degrees, or other recognition); Curation (selectiveness, particular ability in choosing and grouping).
Collaboration: Union (reduces friction between potential competitors by adopting a common set of rules); Alliance (partnership that enables some benefits of a union while maintaining independence); Emergence (hive mind, wisdom of the crowd); Centralization (single decision-making entity); Standardization (emergent alignment that reduces friction); Symbiosis (mutually beneficial dependency between two organisms); Herding (grouping of many individuals to protect against larger or dangerous competitors); Distributed ownership (owned by the community); Transparency (open and visible process or inner workings that encourages trust).
Speed and scale: Iteration (ability to iterate and change quickly); Efficiency (uses a low amount of energy or resources to perform similar capabilities to competitors); Agility (nimbleness, easily adapts to a changing environment); Precision (high level of accuracy and exactness in performance or output); Blitzing (sudden attack, expending intense resources in a concentrated manner); Composability (ability to assemble components and systems in different configurations); Modularity (composed of independent units that can be used in different combinations).
Ease: Intuitiveness (ease of use and understanding, without explicit instruction); Fun (provides enjoyment and amusement); Simplicity (limited points of failure, minimalistic design); Low-friction (minimal resistance or hassle in use or interaction); Charm (charisma, ability to create loyalty and warmth through delightful personality or aesthetics).
Transformation: Malleability (ability to change appearance or form, can be modified or customized easily); Metamorphosis (transforms between states optimized for different functions or environments); Copycat (replicates another's method or style).
